# EQ for balance in three questions: what is the focus, is the clash static or dynamic (sidechained dynamic EQ), and where in the stereo field (mid-only)

Goal: Resolve frequency competition between a focus element (for example a lead vocal) and other loud parts (lead synth, mid bass, piano) with a three-question framework instead of a fixed EQ scoop. (1) Name the section's focus and which parts still compete with it. (2) If the overlap changes with the focus's level, notes and pauses, use a dynamic EQ on the competing part with the focus as its sidechain key, so cuts follow the focus. (3) If the focus is mono but the competitor is stereo, run that dynamic EQ in mid-only mode so the sides keep their full level. Delta vs generic sidechain/EQ skills: the decision order and the mid-only dynamic cut.

Source: [I HATED Mixing Until I Learned This Hack](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2cR4omOL6p4)

1. Set general volume, stereo placement and compression first; EQ for balance comes after those.
2. Ask what the focus of this section is and which other sounds still compete with it (often the parts you chose to mix louder).
3. For each pair, ask whether the overlap is static or dynamic: does it change when the focus pauses, gets louder or quieter, or moves to different notes?
4. For a dynamic clash, insert a dynamic EQ on the competitor and set its sidechain input to the focus track. Adjust the depth (how drastic), resolution (detailed vs broad) and any frequency ranges to ignore.
5. Ask where in the stereo field the clash happens. If the focus is mono (centre) and the competitor is stereo, switch the dynamic EQ to mid-only (or wrap it in M/S encode/decode) so only the centre is ducked.
6. A/B against a static scoop: compare the competitor's level and life in the focus's pauses and in its side channel.

Verify: Metered: during the focus's pauses the competitor has ~0 dB gain reduction; On a side-channel meter, the competitor's side level is unchanged with the processing on and off; The focus stays intelligible in the busiest section at the same fader levels
