# Width by processing left and right differently (L/R EQ, one-sided saturation, formant shift, slap times) with a mono-safe parallel blend

Goal: Widen synths, pads and FX by making the left and right channels genuinely different - double-tracked or alternate sources hard-panned, different L/R EQ, saturation on only one side, a formant-shifted duplicate on one side, and a short slap delay at different times per side (about 50 vs 75 ms) - and blend any phase-risky width (Haas/width plus distortion) in parallel beside an untouched dry chain so mono compatibility is preserved; delta beyond the existing Haas, mid/side and unison skills.

Source: [12 Ways To Widen Your Sounds Like A Pro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z2mgB3LYkgQ)

1. Most organic: make L and R different sources - record an analog synth part twice, or use two different claps/variants - and hard-pan one left and one right.
2. L/R EQ: EQ each side differently (e.g. brighter left; right with highs reduced and mids boosted).
3. Mid/side EQ only helps if the sound already has side content (a single mono output has none): boost sides more than mid.
4. Parallel width chain: keep a dry chain untouched and add a second chain with a Haas/width effect followed by distortion; mix the wet chain in slowly until width appears while mono compatibility stays good.
5. Saturate only one side (pan the drive to the right, leaving the left clean).
6. Chorus as the old-school widener for mono synths - note it can sterilise the tone.
7. Haas via delay (widest but sacrifices phase integrity): one side ~1 ms, the other ~6-20 ms, low feedback, EQ the delay; reserve it for supplemental pads/layers and avoid stacking it on already-reverbed/delayed sounds.
8. Formant-shift one duplicate and leave the other unshifted, panned apart.
9. Slap delay return: about 50 ms on one side and 75 ms on the other (tape-style delay preferred), feedback to taste - cleaner and less unnatural than Haas.
10. Synth-side: two otherwise identical saws with a different waveform variant on one, panned L/R with the random(-phase) control turned down; or unison with per-voice pan and fine detune; or pan different filters per side; reverb also adds width.
11. Check the mono sum after each method and back off the risky ones first.
