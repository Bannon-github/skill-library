# Lydian ambient soundscape layered by frequency range: lower-mid choir chords, mid noise-loop pad, dark saw bass and upper-mid granular/reversed piano sparkles, each checked with a mute test, then arranged by fade-ins, slowly opening filters and an automated reverb mix that merges everything into a cloud

Goal: Build a lush ambient soundscape at 70 BPM in a Lydian key by assigning each layer a frequency range rather than stacking similar pads. Write a 16-bar extended-chord cadence (Cmaj7 → Cadd#11 with the #11 a semitone from the 5th an octave down for gentle dissonance → back to C with the 9th), voice it on a soft choir, bounce it, then filter + EQ + big convolution reverb (lower mids). Add a mid pad: single noise-loop oscillator, soft attack and release, lowpass, a sparser two-note voicing of the same changes an octave up, a slow wandering LFO on noise rate/fade, slight detune spread, tube saturation whose drive is modulated by random noise, a phaser for wash and a huge reverb. Add a dark warm bass (two saws, gradually opening envelope, noise-modulated saturation, chorus, lowpass). Cover the upper mids/highs with a looped granular piano one-shot (small scan, mid grain size, EQ, echo) playing chord tones, bounced and reversed for reverse hits through a big boost EQ, echo and a very large reverb, plus a textured sample through the same chain. Mute-test every layer; keep only the ones you can still hear adding something. Arrange as a slow-evolving piece: bounce layers to audio, fade the intro in, sprinkle unreversed sparkles, slowly open each layer's lowpass over the whole track, and automate the reverb mix on the reversed layer from dry-ish to fully wet so it ends as a sparkly cloud. Polyrhythm Studio: per-layer band roles + long filter/reverb-mix control lanes.

Source: [How to Make Beautiful Ambient Soundscapes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C871ckX4GfQ)

1. Set 70 BPM and a Lydian key (C Lydian: F# instead of F). Write a 16-bar cadence of 4-bar chords: Cmaj7; then drop the 7th and add F# (#11), and move the 5th down an octave so G and F# sit a semitone apart; then the first chord again with the 9th (D) added.
2. Play the cadence on a soft choir/pad, bounce to audio, and add EQ, a lowpass filter (for later automation) and a fully convolution-type, very large reverb so chord changes flow into each other.
3. Mid pad: one noise-loop oscillator (second osc off), soft attack and release, lowpass after it. Copy the MIDI up an octave and thin it to two-note voicings (root+5th, root+3rd, then the D and F# unused elsewhere).
4. Modulate the pad: a slow wandering random LFO on the noise rate and fade, slight detune spread, a tube-type saturation whose drive amount is modulated by a noise (random) source, then a phaser for wash and a very large reverb; optionally an LFO on the filter for stereo movement at a slow rate.
5. Bass: two saw oscillators with a gradually opening filter envelope, saturation with noise-modulated drive, chorus, and a lowpass cutting all high frequencies; follow the chord roots.
6. Upper mids/highs: load a piano one-shot into a granular sampler in loop mode, keep scan small and grain size mid-way (between glitchy and natural), add EQ and echo, and play chord tones (G, F#, D).
7. Bounce the granular part, reverse it, and process with a big EQ boost in the upper mids, echo and a very large dark reverb; run a textured sample through the same chain and bounce it.
8. Mute-test: solo/mute each layer in turn in the full mix; keep only layers you can still hear adding something and that do not muddy others.
9. Arrange: bounce all layers to audio, fade the opening in gradually, add an optional ambient field recording (e.g. rain) underneath, sprinkle short unreversed granular 'sparkles', slowly open every layer's lowpass across the whole track, and automate the reverb mix on the reversed layer from low to fully wet near the end.
