# Cinematic bass FX bed: WT+sub through stacked distortion/compression, then pitch-bend downer

Goal: Build a full cinematic bass/impact bed by pitching a wavetable osc down an octave with subtle bend-warp and width, stacking multiple distortions and compressors so the WT choice matters less than the grind, pushing a sine sub through the same FX, adding early-chain filter/EQ movement into the grit, then reusing the patch for braams/bends/downers via one-shot pitch curves and asymmetric filter envelopes.

Source: [How To Sound Design Cinematic Basses and Subdrops](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PUBMG__czwg)

1. Init WT osc; transpose −12 st. Apply a bend-style warp in small amounts to soften robotic digital WT character. Add modest unison width with reduced detune so it is wide but not chorus-wobbly.
2. Stack several distortions and compressors in series. The more extreme the stack, the less the specific WT matters. Use some compressors to bring high-end back; disable multiband modes if the tone goes boxy.
3. Place a filter or EQ early in the FX chain and modulate a morph/cutoff with one slow LFO plus a second LFO (different rate, limited depth) so movement is smashed into the distortion.
4. Add a second oscillator +12 st with fewer unison voices for air. Keep a sine sub in the patch and intentionally run it through the same FX — bypassing FX shows how much the sub colors the grind; tweak sub pitch for large timbral changes.
5. Post: more compression/saturation (soft-clip/inflator-style with low drive), limiter, enable legato/glide. For braams/bends draw a one-shot pitch curve (e.g. 2 bars). For downers invert the pitch gesture and automate filter cutoff with a fast-then-slow asymmetric curve over ~2 bars, optionally keeping lows less affected.
