# Fast cinematic sketch with only three tracks (piano, full-orchestra patch, synthetic rhythmic swell layer): contrary-motion second melody, transitions made only by automation (low-pass close then open with reverb-mix swells on runs, rhythmic auto-filter dry/wet rising into the chorus) and a tempo ramp of about 95 to 100 to 115 BPM

Goal: Write and arrange a short cinematic cue fast by limiting yourself to three tracks, so you finish the melody, harmony and form before choosing sounds. Without a separate FX track, every section change is made with automation on the existing tracks. This adds the 'three tracks, no FX track' limit and the tempo ramp to the section transition toolkit.

Source: [How to Compose Cinematic Music - FAST](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0Jj5qQQzDLU)

1. Track 1, piano: find two chords that feel emotional going back and forth, add variations, a high ostinato figure on top and a big roll-up. Light low/high EQ lift and a big reverb, then a compressor after the reverb to bring the tail forward.
2. Track 2, full orchestra in one patch: bass instruments play the chord roots. Write a second melody that answers the piano, moving root to third to fifth, and in contrary motion (when the piano goes up, it goes down). Ride expression (CC) for dynamics.
3. End the build with a big string run into the main chorus. In the chorus, the brass/strings carry the main melody with high-woodwind sparkle in the same patch. Voice the sections separately only after the arrangement is done.
4. Track 3, synthetic swell layer for rhythm and texture: tweak attack/ADSR, then put a rhythmic auto-filter/envelope effect on it and automate its dry/wet, low in the intro and full in the chorus.
5. Transitions without an FX track: on the piano run and the orchestra before each new section, automate the low-pass filter closing, then opening as the run lands. Automate reverb mix up during the run for a denser wash. Repeat a smaller close/open later for variation.
6. Tempo automation: start at about 95 BPM, settle at 100, then ramp to about 110-115 BPM during the last run into the main chorus. Keep the new tempo or ease back for the end section.
7. Chorus extras: bring in cinematic drum patterns (in the orchestra patch's percussion layer) and have the synth double the bass roots plus the main melody. Outro mirrors the build: synth goes back to roots only.
8. Master: narrow the lows to mono, EQ plus compression on lows and mids with gentle upward compression on highs for clarity, light bus compression, final EQ and limiter.
