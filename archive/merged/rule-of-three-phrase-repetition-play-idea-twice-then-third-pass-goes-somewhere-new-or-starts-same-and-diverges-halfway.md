# Rule of three for repeating musical ideas: play a 4-bar idea twice so it sticks, then on the third pass either go somewhere new (new melody on the same chords, or new chords) or start the same and diverge halfway

Goal: Stop over-looping a phrase. Play a musical idea (e.g. a 4-bar chord progression with melody) once to spark interest and a second time to reinforce it so the listener can remember it. On the third pass, never repeat it unchanged: option 1, go somewhere different (a new melody over the same chords, or a new progression with a new melody); option 2, start the third pass the same and diverge partway (around halfway through the phrase). Choose the option by intent (the response you want from the listener). Use as a check across a loop-based arrangement.

Source: [This Arrangement Rule Will Change Your Music](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iiL3K3ewfuc)

1. Write or pick a 4-bar idea (progression + melody) and place it once.
2. Repeat it once (2 passes in total) so it is reinforced and memorable.
3. Third pass, option 1: go somewhere different: keep the chords and write a new melody, or move to a new progression with a new melody.
4. Third pass, option 2: start identical to the first two passes, then change the melody or chords about halfway through the phrase.
5. Choose option 1 or 2 by the section's intent and audit the whole arrangement: anywhere an idea plays 3+ times unchanged, apply one of the two options.
