# Build subtractive 'architectures' from operator pairs: six oscillators with their own envelopes, or three oscillator→own-filter blocks (LP / LP+sub / fixed-frequency HP) into a master bandpass, FM pairs into filters, and series HP→LP

Goal: Use a 6-operator engine (or any modular graph) as a configurable subtractive synth in two ways. (A) Six parallel carriers into one master filter, each with its own envelope (VCA before the filter). Layers: two HD saws an octave apart with detune and staggered attacks, a square with LFO-driven PWM, a bright saw with a sharp attack for transient bite, a triangle with width and self-feedback toward saw, and noise whose level is moved by an LFO. Then cutoff, envelope amount, resonance, slight LFO, key tracking, exciter, auto-pan and spring reverb. (B) Turn carriers into filters fed by their modulator to get three independent oscillator→filter blocks: MS-20-style LP with velocity on the envelope; LP with the filter's own sine underneath as a sub; HP switched from key-ratio to fixed frequency for strings, with LFO-driven PWM on the source. All three feed a master bandpass with its own envelope. Then feed FM pairs into the filters for glassy tops, and model classic voice layouts (two oscs in series through HP then LP). The delta vs our multi-envelope level-morph pad is per-oscillator filters, fixed vs key-ratio filter frequency, and FM-into-filter.

Source: [I Tried Building an Analog Synth Inside the Opsix…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X4TPAWQw4ss)

1. Architecture A: choose an algorithm where all six operators are carriers (none modulating each other). Osc 1 = saw (HD/brighter version), osc 2 = saw an octave lower with some detune. Give both full sustain; soften the lower one's attack so it enters more slowly, and add release.
2. Osc 3 = square with an LFO on pulse width (use a spare LFO, set to taste). Osc 4 = bright, higher saw with a sharp attack envelope for bite. Osc 5 = triangle with width adjusted and some self-feedback (pushing it toward saw), used as the lower layer.
3. Osc 6 = noise with a slow envelope, and an LFO on its level so it comes in and out.
4. Master filter: lower cutoff, add filter-envelope amount and resonance, a slight LFO on cutoff and key tracking (cutoff opens up the keyboard). Add exciter, auto-pan and an old-school spring reverb.
5. Architecture B: pick an algorithm with three carrier/modulator pairs. Turn each carrier into a filter (its own oscillator level down) fed by its modulator set to saw. Block 1: MS-20-style low-pass with resonance, cutoff modulated by its envelope with velocity intensity.
6. Block 2: another LP fed by a saw, this time keeping the filter operator's own sine mixed underneath. Because the filter's oscillator follows the pitch section, it can act like a sub oscillator (drop its pitch); detune the source slightly.
7. Block 3: high-pass filter (own mix off) fed by a square, with the filter switched from ratio (key-tracked) to fixed frequency and set by ear for strings, and an LFO on the square's PW.
8. Send all three blocks into the master filter set to band-pass with its own filter envelope.
9. FM into filter: change an algorithm so a modulator stacks on an operator that feeds a filter (sine modulators), giving FM spectra into the analog-style filter and then the master filter (try HP or BP on top). For classic layouts, run two oscillators in series through HP then LP (MS-20-like), or a single oscillator into one filter (Polysix-like).
