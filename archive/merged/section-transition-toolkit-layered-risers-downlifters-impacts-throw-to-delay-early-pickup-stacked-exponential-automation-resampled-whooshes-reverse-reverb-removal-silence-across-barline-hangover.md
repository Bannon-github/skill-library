# Section transition toolkit: layered risers and impacts, throwing a part to a growing delay, a next-section instrument entering early, stacked exponential automation, resampled or reverse-reverb whooshes, removing elements or silence, and transitions that cross or hang over the bar line

Goal: Glue song sections together so they don't feel like 'one thing, then the next thing'. Add transition sounds (risers, whooshes, downlifters; layer and vary them by intensity so they aren't the same every time; synthesize them for control), plus an impact on the landing downbeat (crash or noisy hit into reverb). Make transitions out of existing parts: play the transition (pull back or push harder), throw a vocal or lead into a delay whose mix goes 0 to 100% while feedback grows and a resonant filter sweeps, add upward multiband compression for excitement, and let an instrument from the next section enter early with a pickup. Stack several automations at once (distortion drive, width, delay or grain mix, filter cutoff, envelope decay and release from plucky to ringing) and compensate output gain. Design the end sound first, then automate toward it. Shape curves (exponential by default; hold-then-ramp; turn-back; slam-then-recede). Resample parts and time-stretch or pitch them into custom whooshes; reverse a fully-wet reverb print before the hit. Remove sounds: cut drums or bass, or use a moment of silence. Let transitions cross the bar line (drop instruments for the first beat of the new section) or hang over and fade into it. Combine techniques.

Source: [25 song transition tips I wish I learned from the start](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lnJA5rOrp-M)

1. Pick 1-3 riser, whoosh or downlifter layers sized to the intensity; synthesize at least one (e.g. noise or saw with pitch and filter rising) so length and range fit the boundary. A smaller 'transition to the transition' can lead into a bigger swell.
2. Put an impact on the landing downbeat: a crash, or any noisy hit sent into a big reverb.
3. Throw a lead or vocal to a delay over the last bars: delay mix automated 0 to 100%, feedback rising, a resonant filter sweeping and blending in; optionally add upward multiband compression on the throw.
4. Bring one instrument from the next section in early with a short pickup phrase.
5. Stack automations together: drive on bass, shaper on drums, width, delay or grain mix, filter cutoff, envelope decay/release from short to ringing; compensate with output gain. Build the end sound first, then automate toward it.
6. Shape every curve: exponential (default), hold-then-ramp, turn back at the end, or slam to full then recede.
7. Resample parts of the track through effects, then time-stretch and pitch-automate them into custom whooshes and risers; or duplicate a part to a 100%-wet reverb, print it and reverse it into the hit; or chop audio for a stutter transition.
8. Remove elements: cut drums, bass or atmosphere before the new entries, or insert a short silence before the downbeat.
9. Work across the bar line: drop some instruments for the first beat of the new section, or let a delay or reverb tail hang over and fade under the new section.
10. Combine 2-4 of the above per boundary, scaled to the intensity.
