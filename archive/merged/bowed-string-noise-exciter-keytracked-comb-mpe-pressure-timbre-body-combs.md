# Physically modelled bowed string: key-tracked noise exciters into a 100%-wet key-tracked comb, MPE pressure on gain/resonance, MPE timbre fading in a 3rd-harmonic comb, and a three-comb instrument body that retunes cello to bass, viola or violin

Goal: Build a playable bowed-string voice from synthesis only (no bow sample): a quiet noise exciter feeds a positive comb filter with full key tracking, maximum resonance, 100% wet and cutoff at C4 so it plays in the right octave. MPE pressure (through a shaped response curve) raises exciter gain and lifts comb resonance from about 80 for a scratchy light touch. MPE timbre crossfades in a second key-tracked comb at G5 (third harmonic, resonance ~40%) only near the top of the Y range. A fixed three-comb body (centre a little below 100 Hz, offsets of almost an octave and nearly three octaves, feedback ~15%, tone to taste, pans centred) sets the instrument size. Tame exciter bleed by keeping noise strongest near the played pitch: two S&H-noise oscillators, one optimised for the high end (low cut, key track +10) and one for the low end (high cut, key track −10). Delta beyond our existing noise→resonator and Karplus-style skills: MPE expression mapping plus key-tracked exciter colour and a body comb trio.

Source: [Physically Modeled MPE Strings in Surge XT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9eDmBUC8ZU)

1. Initialise the patch. Route a basic noise oscillator into filter 1 set to comb positive with key tracking full, resonance maximum, 100% wet and cutoff C4. Keep the noise level low and set the overall gain very low before first playing.
2. Add a concert-hall reverb, then set up an MSEG-style response curve driven by MPE pressure (loop off, rate disabled, phase ← pressure ~99%). Use it to raise exciter/output gain and to lift comb resonance from ~80 (unipolar) back toward full.
3. Add filter 2 as a second key-tracked comb at G5 (third harmonic of C4), resonance ~40%, filter mode with two filters in series/blend (S2-type), filter balance fully toward filter 1. Make a second curve named 'timbre' driven by MPE Y (≈49% because it reads as bipolar, base 50%) and shape it so the balance only moves toward filter 2 at the top of the Y range; add pressure to its resonance.
4. Add an instrument body with a three-comb FX block: pans 0, centre a little below 100 Hz (cello), offsets of almost an octave and closer to three octaves, feedback down to ~15%, tone low for a vintage recording sound or higher (or slightly less mix) for 'in the room'. Lower the mix of the upper combs so the centre resonates strongest.
5. Resize the instrument: move the body centre to ~50 Hz and lower noise colour for double bass; raise centre, noise colour and tone by ear for viola and further for violin. Play in the instrument's own range.
6. Tame noise: noise near the comb cutoff resonates nicely, noise far from it sounds like hiss. Key-track the noise colour upward. Better: mute the basic noise and use two S&H-noise oscillators (level ~−10, exciter gain ~−38 then ~−48), tune correlation/width by ear (width can give a formant-like bow character). Oscillator 1 handles high notes (low cut, key track +10) and oscillator 2 handles low notes (high cut, key track −10).
7. Optionally stack a cello-body convolution IR after the combs (watch the low-end build-up and EQ it), then fine-tune levels.
