# Ambient chord arrangement: give each chord tone to a separate instrument (sub root, synth third a tenth up, choir fifth, string doubling the third), each swelling in on its own, moving in parallel and spread wide, at about 55 BPM

Goal: Make a 2-3 chord ambient progression feel like a living choir. Use a slow tempo around 55 BPM (6 s breath cycle) or 60 BPM / halftime. Split each triad across instruments instead of block chords: a sub plays the root of each chord and swells with the mod wheel; a dark synth pad plays the third an octave and more up (a tenth), a choir-like pad adds the fifth above, and a frozen string reinforces the third to define the key. Each part swells in at its own pace and the voices move in parallel. Spread notes apart (avoid close block voicing in the low register to avoid mud): move the third up an octave, the fifth up an octave, or the fifth above the third. Then add reverse textures that move within the key.

5. Instrument 3: choir-like pad plays the fifth above; each instrument swells in uniquely.
6. Instrument 4: frozen string reinforces the third to make the key strong. Move all voices in parallel (all up or all down).
7. Spread the voicing: lift the third or the fifth by an octave, or place the fifth above the third, so the notes are not close together, especially in low registers; add reverse textures in the key.
