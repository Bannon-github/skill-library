# Effects inside an FM operator chain: per-operator EQ/phase/delay/comb/drive/decimator/waveshaper/punch applied to the signal before it modulates or is heard, for delayed-modulator bells, noise-excited comb strings and punchy FM attacks

Goal: Treat each FM operator slot as an optional in-chain processor rather than a master effect, so the processed waveform goes on to modulate the next operator or is heard. Recipes: (1) a peak-EQ or phase (all-pass-like, with feedback) stage on a saw operator, with its frequency moved by mod wheel or LFO, for oscillator-level phasing; (2) a short delay with feedback on the modulator, which gives bells an inner 'chamber' echo when its ratio rises and its time drops, with a short envelope; (3) a comb resonator fed by a very short noise-burst operator, making a physical-model string, copied into three pairs (detuned, an octave up) and finished with chorus, delay and reverb as a 'virtual guitar'; (4) drive, decimator (bit/sample-rate reduction for early 8-bit wavetable tone), waveshaper and 'punch' on a modulator for aggressive FM attacks. Delta vs our KS-pluck, tuned-delay-resonator and FM ratio skills: placing these processors inside the modulation path.

Source: [Opsix Effects Mode: The Secret to New Sounds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XnSE9X0-S9M)

1. Single-operator EQ/phase: put a saw on an operator, switch it to an effect slot with peak EQ (centre frequency + gain) or a phase stage; raise the phase feedback, then map mod wheel or a slow LFO to the effect frequency and/or mix. Mix 0 = only the incoming operator; 100% = only this operator.
2. Delayed-modulator bell: plain FM pair. Set the modulator (operator 2) to a short delay effect and raise its feedback; you hear the delay inside the modulation. Raise the modulator ratio (more harmonics), then shorten the delay time, give it a short envelope and increase the carrier's release.
3. Comb string: carrier operator set to a comb (resonator) effect fed by operator 2. With a sine source it just sounds like overdrive. Switch the source to noise with a very short, clicky envelope, set the mix to only the effect, raise comb feedback for guitar-like tones, lengthen the source envelope for longer strings, and raise the carrier's release.
4. Virtual guitar: copy the comb pair to two more operator pairs (three identical sets). Detune one pair via pitch, and set the third an octave higher with a bit of detune, then combine all three and add chorus, delay and reverb.
5. Drive and distortion on an operator: distortion pushes a sine toward square-like harmonics (mix it back in); 'drive' on a saw acts like an analog oscillator overdriving a VCA/filter, adding subtle warmth.
6. Decimator: reduce bit depth (e.g. 4-bit) and sample frequency on a saw to recreate early 8-bit digital/wavetable-era oscillators. Lower frequency = lower resolution.
7. Waveshaper on a sine or saw operator: choose shaping curves to make wavetable-like waveforms (e.g. a 'super square'), and stack it in the modulation chain as a base layer.
8. Punch on the modulator: set operator 2 to the punch effect and raise it so the FM attack hits harder; useful for aggressive attacking sounds.
