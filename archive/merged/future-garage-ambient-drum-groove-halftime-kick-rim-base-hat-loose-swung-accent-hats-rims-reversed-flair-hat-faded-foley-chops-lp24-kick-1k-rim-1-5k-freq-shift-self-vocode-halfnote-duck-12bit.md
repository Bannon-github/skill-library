# Build a loose future-garage/ambient drum groove in layers: halftime kick and rim, a guide 'base hat' on every 'and', accent hats and rims on hand-swung 16ths (some early, some late, some on the grid), flair hats from one sample with a reversed copy, then faded Foley chops for crunch. Process it with LP24 on the kick (about 1 kHz, 30% resonance) and a band-passed rim (about 1.5 kHz), a frequency shift on the hat group (example -898 Hz), the rims vocoding themselves with chorus, a fast unsynced auto-pan and phaser on Foley (about 6.1 Hz), a half-note volume duck on the hat group, and a 12-bit lo-fi sampler with gentle clip/saturation on the drum bus

Goal: A restrained, organic, swinging drum groove that sits behind ambient pads (lo-fi, in the background, not in-your-face like dubstep/D&B), varied every 2-4 bars so it doesn't sound loopy.

Source: [Future garage / ambient drum patterns, layer by layer, plus post-processing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e4zq3HQdcIY)

1. Core: kick plus main rim in a halftime two-step (one-drop/dub feel), or fulltime with the snare on 2 and near 4.
2. Add a guide 'base hat' on the 'and' of every beat to feel out the spaces. It may be muted in the final mix.
3. Add 2-3 accent-hat layers on other 16ths/8ths. Swing the 2nd and 4th 16ths by hand and not precisely: some hits on the grid, some a little late, some early, until it feels right. Do the same with accent rims for mid-range knock. Group and duplicate the loop, then vary it every 2-4 bars (move hits, swap a sound, stop a layer briefly, a small filter sweep).
4. Make flair hats from one hat sample with velocity changes for bounce, plus a reversed copy placed every so often.
5. Foley crunch: chop a recording (silverware, paper) into single clicks. Line the transients up roughly with a grid value just before the main rim, or on a swung 16th. Fade each chop in and out to soften the transients. Add a transient-heavy 'mid knock' layer (example: celery snaps).
6. Filter the kick with LP24 at 30% resonance and the cutoff around 1 kHz (range 800 Hz-3 kHz depending on the kick). Put the rim through a band-pass/high-pass blend at about 1.5 kHz with a little resonance and filter saturation.
7. Put a frequency shifter on the hat group (example: down 898 Hz), and on other groups as needed. Shifting up gives brighter chill percussion, shifting down gives darker lo-fi. EQ before and after, and re-tune individual elements if one goes weird.
8. Vocoder on the mid-range rim group: use the self-carrier mode (left as carrier, right as modulator) and enable its chorus.
9. Put an auto-pan and phaser at a fast unsynced rate (about 6.1 Hz) on the Foley layer, so each pass of the loop lands differently.
10. Put a volume LFO ducking on half notes over the whole hat/Foley group.
11. Lo-fi: put a 12-bit sampler at a 22.5 kHz sample rate on the hat group. On the drum bus, run a 12-bit/44.1 kHz stage into a hard clipper (about 2 dB drive) and a symmetric soft saturator (about 1.7 dB). Keep it subtle.
