# Live rolling-buffer granular processor with per-grain behaviour modes: interval 'mosaic' clouds, per-grain pitch glide, reverse+wow smear, ghost-grain bloom, LFO tide, fixed-position constellation patterns and orbiting read head

Goal: Turn a constantly recording one-bar buffer into a real-time granular effect for pads, keys, vocals and guitar (insert with dry/wet or 100% wet send). It uses the standard grain controls (size, density, spray around the playhead, pitch in perfect-fifth steps, jitter, spread, stereo scatter, reverse chance, top-end smoothing), a tempo-synced grain trigger/size option, a quantised or auto-moving playhead, and a library of grain-scheduling behaviours a Web Audio granulator can implement: mosaic (grains pitched into chordal interval sets: fifths/major/minor/discordant), glide (each grain sweeps up in pitch over its own length with a curve), reverie (all grains reversed with tape-style wow), bloom (each grain spawns a later, quieter, slightly detuned ghost grain), tide (a global LFO swells and recedes playback rate), stars (grains drawn from fixed buffer positions in forward/reverse/pendulum/random patterns, looping each bar) and orbit (grains circling the write head with width and 'slop'). Delta vs our granular-buffer-core and frozen-buffer skills: live write/read buffer and the per-grain behaviour modes.

Source: [Celestine - A Realtime Granular Dream Machine (Full Walkthrough)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3q-o772IYQY)

1. Insert the processor on a track (balance dry/wet) or on a send at 100% wet. A one-bar ring buffer records whatever plays, and grains are read from it continuously.
2. Set the base cloud: size (grain length), density (grains at once, sparse to dense), spray (how far from the playhead grains may be fetched; 0 = exactly at the playhead), pitch in perfect-fifth steps, jitter (slight per-grain pitch warble, good for cloudy drones), spread (any pitch per grain, extreme), stereo (grains scattered around the field; presenter keeps it high), reverse chance, and smooth (roll off top end if grains get clicky).
3. Playhead: scrub manually, or quantise it to eight positions (eighth notes in the one-bar buffer) to land on downbeat transients, or set a move rate (slow/fast, forward or reverse) to scan automatically.
4. Sync sub-mode: when synced, grains fire on time divisions (e.g. 16ths) and size switches to note lengths. Each behaviour mode below has a free and a synced variant.
5. Mosaic: pitch successive grains into chordal intervals (a couple at root, then +7, +12 …) with a soft bloom and slight lo-fi. Pick an interval set: fifths, major, minor or discordant. Reduce density; add jitter or spread. Good for pads with lots of reverb.
6. Glide: each grain glides up in pitch (micro-portamento). Glide depth sets the interval; grain size sets how long it takes, and a shape control sets the curve. Long grains give a smooth effect; percussive input makes a texture generator.
7. Reverie: force all grains into reverse and add tape-like wow/flutter (wow depth control) with a slight lo-fi tone; longer sizes give stretched tape-echo smears. Reverse chance has no further effect here.
8. Bloom: every triggered grain spawns a secondary ghost grain that enters slightly later, quieter and subtly detuned. Raise density and size for overlapping halos.
9. Tide: a global LFO makes all grains swell and recede in playback rate together. Long size = ambient breathing; short size = tremolo-like.
10. Stars: ignore the heads and read grains from fixed positions in the buffer. Spray becomes a pattern selector (forward, reverse, pendulum, random), which cycles each bar into recognisable melodic or rhythmic fragment loops.
11. Orbit: grains circle the write head in a spiralling orbit. Spray becomes orbit width, plus a slop control for imperfection/lo-fi. Works best at slow tempos and lower densities and plays alongside the dry signal like its own loop.
12. Finish inside the grain path: a wavefolder or bitcrusher with its own mix for grit, then a simple hall reverb (decay + mix); long decay feels almost frozen. Revisit playhead/spray/sync after picking a mode, since each changes the mode's result a lot.
