# Audio-rate wavefolder from scratch: reflect the signal between ±1 (triangle fold) or use a sine fold sin(x·π/2) under up to ~50 dB of input gain, understand where aliasing comes from, choose sources that fold well, and build a small S&H-gain folded wavetable synth

Goal: Implement and use a true wavefolder rather than a clipper. Drive the signal with large input gain (sweep 0 to ~50 dB) and fold every excursion back between the upper and lower full-scale bounds, like two parallel mirrors. Do not reflect off the middle as some bitcrusher 'fold' modes do. Choose between (a) a triangle-shaped transfer function, which leaves the signal untouched until it exceeds full scale but aliases harshly, and (b) a sine fold y = sin(x·π/2), which colours slightly from the start but aliases far less at high gain. Feed it sines (rich, musical), not saws (alias and wrap), white noise (no audible change) or full guitar (harsh inharmonic aliasing); drums work around +30 dB. Turn it into a folded wavetable voice: a 512-sample single-cycle table at 48 kHz (93.75 Hz), phase reset so both ends sit at zero, sample-and-hold random gain 8× per second, a random MIDI note on every tenth gain change, and the phase ramp as a crude decay envelope. This fills our audio-rate wavefolder gap, beyond the wavefolded-LFO and complex-osc fold skills.

Source: [EXTREME WAVE FOLDING: a Pure Data experiment.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G3CIJ3l5Fd8)

1. Build a test oscillator (e.g. a 200 Hz cosine), scale the output down (~0.2) or use a volume-controlled output, and add a scope/array view so you can see the waveform.
2. Add an input gain stage that can go far beyond full scale (sweep 0 → ~50 dB over ~10 s for testing). Note that plain clipping only flattens the peaks.
3. Triangle fold: map the overdriven signal through a triangle-shaped transfer curve so excursions reflect between +1 and −1 repeatedly (build it from absolute value and a modulo). Use a modulo/remainder that behaves correctly for negative numbers; a C-style fmod breaks it. The signal stays untouched until it exceeds full scale.
4. Sine fold alternative: y = sin(x·π/2) (a sine transfer with period 4). It adds a little distortion even at low gain but much less aliasing at high gain. Prefer it for musical use.
5. Test sources: sine sweeps sound interesting; a saw becomes triangles and then 'wraps around' (aliasing); white noise is unchanged; a guitar gives irregular, obnoxious inharmonic peaks; a drum beat at ~+30 dB is a usable trashy texture. Compare against plain clipping.
6. Folded wavetable voice: write one folded cycle into a 512-sample table (power-of-two period; at 48 kHz that is 93.75 Hz). Reset the oscillator phase (0.25 to turn cosine into sine) so the cycle starts and ends at zero before applying gain.
7. Generative control: sample-and-hold white noise on a phase ramp to apply a random fold gain 8 times per second, trigger a random MIDI note (mtof) on every tenth gain change, and use the phase ramp as a crude amplitude envelope (each note starts louder and ends quieter).
