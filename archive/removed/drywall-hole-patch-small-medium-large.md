# Patch a drywall hole (small, medium, large)

Goal: Repair a hole in drywall so it is invisible after painting, choosing the method by hole size.

Source: [How to Patch a Drywall Hole | Ask This Old House](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PLGmTzEGSIY) (This Old House)

1. Size the hole. Small = nail or picture-hanger holes; medium = doorknob or baseboard dings; large = something went through the wall.
2. Small: press spackling compound into the hole with a putty knife and smooth it flush. Let it dry; it shrinks a little, so apply another coat or two as needed, then sand smooth.
3. Medium: cover the hole with heavy-duty mesh patching tape (use about three layers) or an adhesive-backed metal mesh patch that overlaps sound wall on all sides. Press it flat.
4. Medium: spread a thin, smooth coat of spackling compound over the patch. Let it dry, add a second smooth coat, and sand; add a third coat and sand again if it is not smooth yet.
5. Large: cut a square drywall patch first, trace it onto the wall over the damage, and cut the wall opening out to the traced line, slightly bigger than the patch so it fits.
6. Large, option A (backing): slide a wood board behind the opening and screw it to the wall on each side, or fit drywall repair clips on two or four edges; screw the patch to the board or clips, remove any temporary screws, and hammer the clip tabs flat.
7. Large, option B (paper-flange patch): cut the drywall patch bigger than the hole, then snap and peel away the gypsum around the perimeter so only the face paper remains as a flange; trim the core so it fits the opening with a little play.
8. For option B, butter the wall around the opening with compound (joint compound for anything bigger than a medium hole), press the patch in, press the paper flange around the perimeter, and scrape excess away from the seam with the knife held upright. Let it set.
9. Apply second and third thin coats, each as smooth as possible and feathered wider than the last, letting each dry.
10. Sand and feather the edges, then verify by eye (and a raking light) that the patch is invisible and has no cracks before priming and painting.
