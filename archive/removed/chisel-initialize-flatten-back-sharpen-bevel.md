# Initialize and sharpen a new chisel

Goal: Prepare a new chisel for work and keep it sharp afterward.

Source: [How to Sharpen a Chisel | Paul Sellers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GN4yr7vp4I4) (Paul Sellers)

1. Inspect the new chisel: visible grinding marks on the bevel mean it is not refined, even if it cuts.
2. Use flat sharpening plates/stones (or abrasive paper on plate glass if stones are not flat).
3. Flatten the back only near the cutting edge on the coarse stone, keeping it dead flat; a hollow behind the edge is fine and need not be removed.
4. Repeat on the medium and superfine (about 1200 grit) stones until the coarser striations are gone.
5. Charge a wood block with steel buffing compound and rub the back flat on it until the edge area shines.
6. Bevel: feel for the factory grind angle and push/pull at that angle on the coarse stone (a slight camber is acceptable) until you feel a burr across the full width on the back.
7. Repeat on medium, then spend longer on superfine to remove earlier scratches.
8. Flip and pull the back flat once on the stone to remove the burr.
9. Charge the leather strop with compound; place the bevel flat, heel of hand on the chisel, and pull about 30 strokes; strop the back briefly to clear any burr.
10. Test on wood for a clean, untorn surface. Routine resharpening: repeat only the bevel steps and just break the burr on the back.
