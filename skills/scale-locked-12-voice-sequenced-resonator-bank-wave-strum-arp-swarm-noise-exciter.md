# Scale-locked resonator bank as an instrument: up to 12 tuned resonator voices on a root and scale (e.g. D minor 9, 6 voices, octave 3), excited by a stereo noise burst or drums, with motion modes (wave, strum, arpeggio, swarm), feedback/decay/damping/spread/diffusion and gated live-key voicing

Goal: Use a bank of tuned resonators as a playable texture generator. Tune all voices to a root note and scale chord (example: root D, minor 9, 6 voices in octave 3) and pick a resonator model (string, bar, glass harmonica, bowl, membrane, bell, plate, tube; strings add position and brightness). Excite it with a short stereo white-noise burst (ADSR with no sustain, two steps), kicks, snares, hats, pads or piano. Animate which voices sound with a motion mode: wave (amplitude, per-voice frequency spread, phase, rate), strum (spread, curvature, direction up/down/alternate, alternate rate), arpeggio (rate, gate, up/down/up-down/random) or swarm (centre, cohesion, rate). Shape the tail with feedback, decay, damping, stereo spread and diffusion, plus vibrato, and switch to live keys or gated mode to set voices from held chords. More specific than our single harmonic-resonator and noise-crackle skills: a multi-voice scale bank with voice-motion sequencing.

Source: [Tessera by Audio Damage (Tuned Sequenced Resonator)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aPsWzEfgEOo)

1. Insert the resonator bank on a source. Set root (lockable) and scale, then the number of voices and octave (example D minor 9, 6 voices, octave 3).
2. Choose a resonator model; test kick, snare and hats through it, trimming output when loud. On string models also set position and brightness.
3. Build a noise exciter: an ADSR-gated stereo white-noise generator with no sustain, triggered on two steps of a sequencer.
4. Pick a motion mode. Wave: adjust amplitude, per-voice frequency spread (more chaotic), phase and rate. Strum: spread, curvature, direction (one way, opposite, alternating) and alternate rate. Arp: rate, gate, up/down/up-down/random. Swarm: centre, cohesion (tight to loose) and rate. Set steps and window size, tempo-synced if wanted, and randomise.
5. Shape the tail: feedback, decay, damping, stereo spread, diffusion; add vibrato rate/depth; balance dry/wet.
6. Switch to live keys so the held notes set the voices (one note, two notes, four notes), or gated mode to sound only while keys are held. Try a pad or piano as input with mix fully wet.
