# Add drums to a gorgeous slow pad track without crushing it: map the track's accents first, put kicks only in bar 1 (plus one ghost note) of each 4-bar phrase, snares on bars 2 and 4 with a big reverb only on the second round and a reversed-reverb swell before bar 4's downbeat, view the grid at double tempo (67 to 134 BPM) to find off-kick hat spots, alternate two hats' brightness with a 2-bar LFO on an EQ, add a ride in only two places, and let a quiet high-passed noise bed seep into the gaps, ducked by the percussion with about a 3 s release

Goal: Sparse drums that leave space for the pads, with variation across a 4-bar phrase instead of a busy repeating rock pattern (the 'Walk This Way' kick-snare-kick-kick-snare trap the student fell into).

Source: [Spacious ambient / lo-fi drums that still have character](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcbSwdsC7W8)

1. Listen through the pad track and mark the accents (example: a big block chord at bars 9 and 13) and the space at the end of each 4-bar phrase. Decide per phrase whether that space stays empty for the listener or gets busier percussion.
2. Kicks: put them in bar 1 of the 4-bar phrase only, plus one ghost note just before the repeat. Roll off the very low end. Put a compressor on the pad group, sidechained to the kick, so the pads gain a gentle bounce.
3. Snares: put them on bars 2 and 4. On the second round only, send to a big lush reverb (EQ on the return). Print that reverb, reverse it and place the swell only before the downbeat of bar 4, not bar 1, so it doesn't get monotonous. Add a short widening delay to the swell.
4. Shorten the snare and rim-shot tails: warp the snare in Beats mode, preserve transients, loop-forward, and set the envelope to 0 so it rings out less.
5. Temporarily double the project tempo (example 67 to 134 BPM) so the click shows subdivisions. Place two hats outside the kick hits, slightly ahead of the bar line, then go back to the real tempo.
6. Put an LFO on an EQ's frequency, alternating between about 80 and 100 (as spoken) at a 2-bar rate, so the second hat is always the darker one, like clockwork, without drawing automation.
7. Make the main hat/percussion line soft and crackly. Apply a swing groove to the audio (a jazz 5/4 swing template), then pull the transient amount right down. Automate a stereo delay's width so the high notes are wide and the low ones go mono. Glue-compress the peaks and use gain automation to level the quiet hits.
8. Add drummer character: a ride only at the end of bar 3 and straight on the downbeats of bars 7-8, a few panned glass Foley hits, and chopped toms. Saturate and low-cut the toms and lower their transients.
9. Swap a stock cymbal for a cymbal/organic swirl through delay, large-hall reverb and a vocoder. Automate the vocoder, reverb and delay dry/wet and fade it in.
10. Noise bed: run a noise generator through a ducking envelope (depth about x8, threshold low, release about 3 s), keyed by the percussion and high-passed. Set it very low, about -13 to -17 dB, so noise seeps into the gaps about 3 s after percussion stops.
