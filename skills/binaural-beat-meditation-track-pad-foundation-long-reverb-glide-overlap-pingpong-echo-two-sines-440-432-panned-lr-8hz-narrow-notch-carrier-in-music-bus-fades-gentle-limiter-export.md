# Produce a binaural-beat meditation track: a two-chord pad foundation with long reverb and overlapping glided notes, a sparse echoing piano and subtle bass, volume-automated arrangement, then a binaural layer of two pure sines (440 Hz and 432 Hz for an 8 Hz difference) panned hard left/right, a narrow high-Q EQ cut at the carrier frequency on the music bus so nothing masks it, fades, a gentle limiter (~3 dB input, no audible gain reduction) and WAV/MP3 export at 44.1 kHz

Goal: Make the whole track, not just the beat. Start from pads: play a simple two-chord loop (around A and D), add reverb with high decay and wet mix, keep chord changes on the grid, and let notes overlap with glide so transitions slide instead of stepping. Add a short piano line with an echo (more feedback, ping-pong on) and its own reverb, then a subtle low bass on the same roots; pull all levels down to avoid overdrive. Arrange by drawing volume automation so elements enter and leave (less is more; one element can carry minutes), boost the highs with a high shelf on bright parts, put delay before reverb. Binaural layer: a sine at the root frequency (A = 440 Hz) and a duplicate at 432 Hz (8 Hz difference); unpanned they make an audible wobble (monaural beat), panned hard left and right they form the binaural beat. Group the music and cut a narrow notch (high Q) at 440 Hz so the instrumental doesn't overlap the carrier. Fade the beat in and out, keep it quiet but present when muted/unmuted. On the master, add a limiter on the loudest section with only ~3 dB input and no audible gain reduction; export 44.1 kHz WAV plus an MP3, then do a calm test listen later and adjust.

Source: [Producing a Binaural Beats Meditation Track in Ableton Live (Step-By-Step)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GndBM5EifHA)

1. Pick a soft pad, play a simple two-chord loop (e.g. A then D), add reverb with high decay and wet mix, capture the MIDI and align chord changes to the grid; loop it.
2. Layer a second pad for texture and a sparse piano phrase; add an echo with more feedback and ping-pong, plus a reverb with moderated wet mix.
3. Lengthen notes so they overlap at chord changes and enable glide so pitch slides between them.
4. Add a subtle low bass on the roots; lower all instrument levels to avoid clipping.
5. Arrange with volume automation: bring elements in and out gradually; high-shelf boost and mid cut on bright parts; put delay before reverb.
6. Binaural layer: a sine instrument at 440 Hz and a duplicate at 432 Hz; listen unpanned (wobble), then pan one hard left and one hard right; program one note across the whole track.
7. Group the music tracks and add a narrow, high-Q EQ cut at 440 Hz on the group.
8. Fade the beat in and out; set its level so it is quiet but noticeably different when muted.
9. On the master, loop the loudest section and add a limiter with about 3 dB input and no audible gain reduction; export WAV 44.1 kHz and an MP3; test-listen later in a calm setting and adjust.
