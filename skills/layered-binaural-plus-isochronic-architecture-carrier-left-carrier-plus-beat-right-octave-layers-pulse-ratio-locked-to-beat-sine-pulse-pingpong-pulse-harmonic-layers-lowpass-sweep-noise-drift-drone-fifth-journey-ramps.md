# Layered binaural + isochronic track architecture: carrier in the left ear and carrier+beat in the right (e.g. 100/104 or 100/110 Hz), octave-up/down copies for depth, an isochronic pulse ratio-locked to the beat rate (1:1, 1:2, 2:1) with sine pulse shape, optional left-right ping-pong pulsing, extra harmonic/anchor tone layers pulsed at the same rate, a slow lowpass sweep that fades harmonic layers in and out, panned drifting pink/white/brown noise atmosphere, a drone a fifth below, and a 'journey' of timed beat-rate segments with smooth ramps (e.g. 10 → 1.5 Hz descent)

Goal: Design richer Mind Amend-style tracks than a bare two-tone beat. Binaural: one pure tone per ear, the right ear tone = carrier + beat (100 Hz left, 104 Hz right gives a 4 Hz beat; removing one ear leaves a steady tone). Isochronic: the same rate as on/off pulsing of a tone; both can play together. Choose any comfortable carrier (100 Hz in the demo, with micro-tuning), add octave layers (½× and 2× carrier) for depth, and pulse layers at a rate locked to the beat by a simple ratio (1:1, half, double) so nothing drifts out of sync when the beat changes; sine pulse shape is preferred. Ping-pong pulse alternates the pulse left/right instead of on/off. Extra harmonic tones (the presenter uses solfeggio-labelled frequencies and says they lack scientific basis) or a fifth/12th harmonic stack are pulsed at the same rate. A lowpass sweep (period ~6 s and longer) fades harmonic layers in and out so no tone is constant. Pink/white/brown noise as wind/ocean air, auto-panned. A low drone a fifth below supports the field. A journey timeline holds a beat rate for set minutes, then glides smoothly to the next (5 Hz 5 min → 2 Hz → 1 Hz; sleep descent 10 → 1.5 Hz; a 12 → 4.5 Hz ramp). Polyrhythm Studio: one shared rate LFO drives beat offset and every pulse lane via integer ratios.

Source: [Binaural Beats Are Boring… So I Built Something Else | Bisochronic® Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bFaYEPLlIxQ)

1. Set the carrier (e.g. 100 Hz pure sine) in the left ear and carrier + beat in the right (e.g. 104 Hz for 4 Hz). Check with headphones: removing one side leaves a steady tone.
2. Add octave copies at half and double the carrier at lower level.
3. Add an isochronic pulse with sine shape on a layer at ratio 1:1 to the beat; try half and double. Change the beat and confirm the pulse follows.
4. Optional ping-pong: pulse the layer left/right instead of on/off and raise depth.
5. Add harmonic/anchor tones (e.g. a fifth or 12th above, or chosen fixed tones) pulsed at the same rate or left steady.
6. Put a slow lowpass sweep on the harmonic layers (start ~6 s period, then longer) with adjustable intensity.
7. Add a low pink/white/brown noise layer with slow auto-pan, and a low drone a fifth below.
8. Build a journey: hold each beat rate for set minutes and glide to the next (e.g. 5 Hz 5 min → 2 Hz 5 min → 1 Hz; or a 10 → 1.5 Hz descent); export and keep a card listing every frequency and ratio.
