# Manual reverse-reverb swell: reverse, print 100%-wet reverb, re-reverse, trim and fade into the onset

Goal: Make a reverse-reverb swell by hand (no dedicated plugin) that leads into a vocal, chord or hit with its own timbre: duplicate and reverse the source, print a large 100%-wet reverb of the reversed audio, keep only the part that leads into the first word/hit, reverse it back so it swells forward, normalize, align and fade it into the dry onset, and optionally give the swell its own late-automated reverb tail.

Source: [Reverse Reverb Technique, Tips and Tricks in Cubase Music Production Tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DpXw9UPrTDU)

1. Duplicate the source audio onto a second track.
2. Reverse the duplicate (audio process -> reverse).
3. Insert a large reverb (large live-stage/hall size) on that track with mix at 100% so you hear only the wet signal.
4. Bounce/export the soloed wet signal to a new audio file (mono in the source example).
5. Keep only the part that matters - the portion that leads into the first word/hit - and delete the rest and the temporary track.
6. Reverse the printed clip back so the reverb now swells forward into the onset, and normalize it.
7. Move it to just before the dry source and add short fade-in/fade-out at the clip edges to remove clicks.
8. Optional: put another reverb (~50% mix) on the swell channel so it has a softer tail, and automate that reverb to come in only at the last split second so the whole tail does not wash out.
9. Reuse on other sources: kick, cymbal crash into a breakdown, white-noise rise, pads/chords.
