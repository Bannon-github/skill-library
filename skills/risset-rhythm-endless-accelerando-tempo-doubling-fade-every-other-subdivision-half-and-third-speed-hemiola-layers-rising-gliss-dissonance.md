# Endless-accelerando (Risset rhythm) illusion for cinematic tension: the same pattern at double speed fading out and at half speed fading in while tempo automates to double, dropped subdivisions (fade every other note of a six-pulse group as tempo doubles), half-speed (3/4) and third-speed (6/8) copies forming a 3:2 hemiola fractal, voices swapping roles across cycles, and a long note gliding slowly against a sustained pitch

Goal: Make a rhythm that seems to speed up forever without ever getting too fast, the rhythmic version of a Shepard tone. Basic form: play a pattern, the same pattern at double speed fading out above it, and at half speed fading in below it, while tempo automates up to double; then loop. In a track: take a group of six steady pulses and, as tempo rises toward double, fade every second note until it disappears, so at double tempo you hear the original rhythm again. Layer the same six-pulse pattern at half speed (three strong pulses, 3/4 feel) and at one-third speed (two pulses, 6/8 feel) for a 3:2 hemiola; give each layer its own dropped-subdivision transformation and fade layers in and out across cycles so the ear never locks onto one, and let a voice that played the 2-pulse role take the 3-pulse role in the next cycle. Double tempo per cycle (150 → 300 → 600 in the video) and reset where the DAW tempo ceiling is reached. Add tension with a long note rising slowly against a sustained pitch, passing every dissonance. 2. Dropped subdivisions: play a group of six pulses; as tempo rises toward double, ramp down the level of every second note until silent.
3. Add the same six-pulse pattern at half speed (three strong pulses, 3/4 feel) and at one-third speed (two pulses, 6/8 feel).
4. Apply the dropped-subdivision fade to each layer in turn, staggering them, and fade whole layers in and out across cycles; let a voice move from the 2-pulse role to the 3-pulse role in the next section.
5. Automate tempo doubling cycle by cycle (e.g. 150 → 300 → 600) and reset to the start tempo at a cycle boundary when you hit the tempo ceiling, with fades placed in those transition zones.
