# Turn bright percussive or decaying instruments (electric piano, vibraphone, piano, glockenspiel) into an evolving ambient pad by running each through 100% wet reverbs of about 20-40 s (chaining two reverbs for longer tails), bit-crushing the piano before the reverb to sustain and add harmonics, taming harsh highs with EQ, then busing the layers with a low cut, compression, sidechain and a filter that reveals the pad

Goal: Make an ambient pad from stock instruments rather than a synth. Pick sources with rich, bright harmonics (distorted electric piano, vibraphone, piano, glockenspiel) and play the same chords stacked across several octaves, panning some layers slightly left and right. Put each through a reverb set to fully wet with no dry signal and a very long decay (about 33 s, 22 s, 40 s in the video); daisy-chain two algorithmic reverbs to get even longer tails and lower the level. The reverb smears the transients away and effectively adds a long attack. For extra brightness and sustain, bit-crush (distort) the piano before its reverb, then EQ down the harsh upper-mid/top it added. Roll off some highs on very bright sources like glockenspiel before the reverb. Because the layers use different reverb lengths they create multiple attack phases, so the sound evolves. Bus all layers; EQ out the large low-frequency/sub-20 Hz build-up from the long reverbs, add compression, sidechain compression and a low-pass filter that you automate open to introduce the pad.

Source: [How to make an ambient pad in Logic Pro X - Trance Production Tutorials #10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mnledZoD4QE)

1. Program a couple of chords stacked across several octaves; load bright sources (distorted electric piano, vibraphone, piano, glockenspiel) on separate tracks, panning a few slightly left or right.
2. On the electric piano, insert a reverb at 100% wet / 0% dry with about 33 s decay.
3. On the vibraphone, daisy-chain two algorithmic reverbs (fully wet) for an even longer tail; turn the track down.
4. On the piano, insert bit crusher → EQ → reverb (fully wet, about 22 s); crush fairly hard to sustain and add harmonics, then cut the harsh upper-mid/top with EQ.
5. On the glockenspiel, roll off some of the top with EQ before a fully wet reverb of about 40 s (chained reverbs).
6. Listen to all layers together: different decays give multiple attack phases that morph over time.
7. Route all layers to a bus: EQ out the low/sub build-up, add compression and sidechain compression, and a low-pass filter.
8. Automate the bus filter open to introduce the pad in the arrangement.
