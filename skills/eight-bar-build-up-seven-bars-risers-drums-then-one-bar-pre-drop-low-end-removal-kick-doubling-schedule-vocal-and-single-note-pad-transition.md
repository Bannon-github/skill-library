# Eight-bar build-up template: seven bars of risers, drums and sustained parts, one bar of pre-drop with everything cut, low end automated away, kick doubling schedule, filtered-in snare, single-note pad with pitch rise, and a readable vocal before the drop

Goal: Build a 4/4 build-up before a drop. Structure: 8 bars (16 bars for drum and bass). Seven bars of risers, drums and sustained instruments; the final bar is the pre-drop where everything else cuts off (snare roll, pre-drop vocal, sub build). Transitions: into the build-up use quiet up-risers and down-filters starting at its first bar; take one note of a chord-progression pad, hold it for the build, and automate pitch up over the last four bars; use vocals that actually say something in the last four bars (chop only the first four); thin the drums before the build if there is no breakdown, filter them in gradually if there is. Into the drop: always include one pre-drop bar, gradually automate low end out of bass, pads and risers, and optionally dip the loudest elements (not vocals) just before the drop. Elements: kick every beat for 4 bars, doubled for 2, doubled again for 1 (and through the last bar if the vocal matches), filtering out as it goes; a snare every step, filtering in from already half-open, plus a second low snare after 4 bars; hats/claps/percussion loop for depth; bring back an element from the intro/breakdown as a sustained part with a riser; layer multiple quiet risers.

Source: [3 Step Guide To Make The Perfect Build Up | Structure, Transitions & Core Elements](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nkNSlRVGb38)

1. Set the length: 8 bars for 4/4 styles, 16 bars for faster drum and bass. Typical layout: 16-bar intro, optional 8-bar breakdown, 8-bar build-up, drop.
2. Bars 1-7: risers, drums and sustained instruments; bar 8: pre-drop where everything else cuts off (snare roll, pre-drop vocal, sub build). Do not skip the pre-drop bar unless the groove rolls straight into the drop.
3. Start quiet up-risers and down-filters at bar 1 of the build-up so the transition into it carries momentum; layer several risers, all low in level.
4. Take one note of the previous chord pad, hold it for the whole build-up, keep it static for four bars, then automate pitch rising over the last four bars.
5. Vocals: chop them in the first four bars; make the last four bars (pre-drop) say something recognisable. If a full vocal runs through the track, skip chops.
6. Drums: with no breakdown, remove some drums in the transition so the return hits harder; with a breakdown, filter elements in gradually instead. Kick: every beat for 4 bars, double time for 2 bars, double again for 1 bar (continue through the pre-drop bar if the vocal matches), filtering out as it goes.
7. Snare: a hit every step, filtered in starting already half-open; after 4 bars add a second low snare; add hats, claps and a percussion loop for movement. Stop all drums at the pre-drop.
8. Remove low end as you approach the drop: automate bass, pads and risers' low frequencies down gradually. Optionally dip the loudest non-vocal elements (instruments/drums) rather than the master if vocals are present.
