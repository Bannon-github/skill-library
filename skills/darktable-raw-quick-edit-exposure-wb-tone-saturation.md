# darktable quick RAW edit: exposure, white balance, tone, saturation

Goal: Turn a RAW photo into a usable image in darktable with four modules.

Source: [How to edit any RAW photo using Darktable in 7 minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rngo-yvvNdA) (Brandon Cy)

1. Drag the RAW file into darktable (lighttable).
2. Open preferences (gear icon) > processing and confirm the workflow is "scene-referred (sigmoid)".
3. Double-click the photo to enter the darkroom; click the active-modules icon to see what darktable already enabled.
4. Exposure module: move the exposure slider until the main subject or focal point looks right.
5. White balance: leave the white balance module alone and use color calibration. Keep its automatic default, or set illuminant to "as shot in camera", "daylight" (adjust temperature), or "custom" (creative tint), or use the eyedropper (gray-world box; a box around the whole frame gives a neutral result).
6. Contrast: search for and enable tone equalizer; with it open, hover over an image area and scroll the mouse wheel to brighten or darken that tonal range (e.g. lower shadows, pull highlights slightly to restore sky color). The advanced-tab curve updates to match.
7. Saturation: open color balance rgb, master tab. Use vibrance for subtle global colorfulness, and the global/shadows/midtones/highlights saturation sliders to fix specific ranges (e.g. lower midtone and highlight saturation on oversaturated reds).
8. Refine: arrow keys or right-click a slider to type a value; double-click a slider to reset it; module reset button resets the module; Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes; toggle modules for before/after. Modules apply bottom-up, so revisiting exposure later keeps the other modules' settings.
9. Export when satisfied.
