# Solder a through-hole joint in five steps

Goal: Make a reliable through-hole solder joint on a circuit board.

Source: [Soldering Tutorial for Beginners: Five Easy Steps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qps9woUGkvI) (oneTesla)

1. Gather a soldering iron, a wet or brass sponge, rosin-core solder, and optionally helping hands; wear safety goggles and treat all metal near the iron as hot.
2. Set the iron to 315-370 C (600-700 F) for through-hole parts; go slightly hotter for large copper areas that sink heat.
3. Secure the part: bend the leads outward in a V so it sits flush against the board, or tape parts without long leads.
4. Clean the tip on the sponge to remove oxidation and old solder; optionally tin the tip with a tiny bit of fresh solder for heat transfer.
5. Press the flat side of the tip against both the pad and the lead, and feed solder from the opposite side onto the joint (not onto the iron). Allow longer for larger joints.
6. Remove the solder first, then the iron; keep the joint still for a few seconds while it cools.
7. Inspect: the solder should wet both pad and lead, look smooth and shiny, and form a volcano shape, with neither too much nor too little solder. Rework if not.
8. Trim excess leads. Avoid: carrying a solder blob on the iron to the joint, touching the joint, and misaligned parts.
