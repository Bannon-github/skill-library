# Three section transitions with no big risers or snare rolls, for simple two-chord tracks: (1) the lead plays alone while the pad's filter closes, a simple sweep and a lead variation, then kick, hats and bass drop in; (2) keep open hats, filter the pad down, put a clap on the beat with no kick and a high-passed bass, loop a lead variation, then one quarter-note tom hit opens the drop with the bass filter removed; (3) strip to the hat loop, tease lead variations (velocity, pan, spacing) with no kick or bass, then a very short fill and everything returns at once

Goal: Make transitions by subtraction and re-entry instead of piling on FX. Simple material needs simple transitions. The kick-plus-bass re-entry is the payoff, and the kick acts as the track's metronome that listeners wait for. A riser-free counterpart to the section transition toolkit and the three-track automation sketch.

Source: [3 SIMPLE transitions WITHOUT using risers or snare roll build ups](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vzlZ4GabrAs)

1. Intro to first drop: run the intro with just the kick and a closed-hat drum loop, and the pad opening and closing on a filter. At the end, let the pluck/lead play on its own with the pad filter coming down, a simple sweep and a lead variation as the last bar.
2. Land the first drop with kick, closed hats, open hats and the bassline entering together.
3. Mid transition, part 1: play a lead variation, then return to the original. Keep the open hats, low-pass the pad steadily down, mute kick and closed hats, add a clap on the beat and high-pass the bassline.
4. Mid transition, part 2: loop the lead variation and let the clap settle. Place one tom hit (about a quarter note long) in the last beat's space before the downbeat.
5. Re-enter all drums with the second lead variation, open hats and the bass with its filter removed, so the low end hits with the kick.
6. Final payoff: strip back to the closed-hat loop and open hats with the pad dipping again, with no kick and no bass. Tease the lead with its variations (same notes, different velocity, pan and spacing, for example one extra bar of space).
7. End the tease with a very short drum fill (sweep plus clap/snare), then bring everything back at once: drums, bass, pad and lead.
