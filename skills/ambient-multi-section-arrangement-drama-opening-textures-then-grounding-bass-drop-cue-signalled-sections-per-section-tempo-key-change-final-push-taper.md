1. Open with the first three or four texture patches (pads plus a modulated square-wave 'electric' texture with reverb and delay) to create drama before any drums.
2. Resample the square-wave textures and glitch them in a sampler/glitch device as a high-frequency 'afterthought' layer; bring it back later so it is not forgotten.
3. Ground the texture bed with a simple four-bar bass hit, then keep building (extra pads layered on top) toward a crescendo.
4. At the first drop (about bar 56) release the tension: a whoosh/boom sample, then the bass line, sidechained to the kick.
5. Signal each new section with a small cue (tiny hats entering, snare, whoosh) so the listener subconsciously hears 'change is coming'; omit the cue when the new part is soft enough to stand on its own.
6. Change tempo in the master tempo lane at section boundaries (e.g. 131 → 111 → 136 BPM) and let a new bass line or pad define the new section.
7. Final push: hat enters, bass line changes, lower the competing pad about 4-5 dB so another pad has room; then taper — fade the bass out and leave the inner atmosphere.
