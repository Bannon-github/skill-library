# Classic house pad in any synth: minor 9 / minor 7 / major 7 chords, many-voice detuned unison with oscillator retrigger off (free-running), stereo spread from the unison width control or a chorus/ensemble if the synth has none, slightly resonant lowpass plus serial highpass to remove mud, slow LFO on cutoff, slow amp attack with no filter envelope, coloured noise partly bypassing the lowpass, delay + reverb sends; plus a quick pad from a looped chord-stab sample

Goal: Build the classic house pad and know which ingredient does what. Use extended chords (minor 9, minor 7, major 7, major 9 and inversions). Turn unison up (up to 16 voices per oscillator, or 32), detune until it is wide and airy but not seasick, and give a second oscillator a different detune so voices do not sit on top of each other. Turn oscillator retrigger off so voices run free; with retrigger on, voices start in phase and just phase against each other. If unison does not spread voices in stereo, use the width control or add a chorus (an ensemble chorus suits pads). Set a lowpass cutoff a little laid-back with some resonance for character, cut the lows with a highpass (in series), and move cutoff with a slow LFO. Use a slow amp attack and full sustain, and remove any filter envelope. Try pulse waves and PWM by LFO, a sub oscillator for weight, and coloured noise at low level fed partly around the lowpass so its highs survive. Finish with delay and reverb sends. Quick alternative: loop a steady section of a chord-stab sample with a crossfade and slow attack, then hide the loop with delay, reverb and a filter.

Source: [How to Create Classic House Pads (Any Synth!)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nbS1pT8V7YU)

1. Program extended chords (e.g. Em9, Em7, Emaj7) on an init saw patch with the filter open.
2. Raise unison toward 16 voices and detune until wide and airy; add oscillator 2 with its own unison at a slightly different detune.
3. Turn off oscillator retrigger so unison voices free-run.
4. If voices are not spread in stereo, raise the unison width/spread control; if the synth has none, add a chorus/ensemble after it.
5. Set the lowpass cutoff a little low with some resonance; add a highpass in series to remove muddy lows; add a slow LFO on cutoff.
6. Set a slow amp attack with full sustain and remove filter-envelope amount.
7. Optional colour: switch to pulse or mix pulse and saw, modulate pulse width with a slow LFO, add a sub oscillator; add coloured noise at low level, routing some of it straight to the highpass/output so its highs bypass the lowpass.
8. Add delay and reverb on sends and check against drums.
9. Chord-stab variant: load a chord stab into a sampler, loop a steady section after the transient (snap off), add loop crossfade and slow attack, then hide the repeat with delay, reverb and a filter.
