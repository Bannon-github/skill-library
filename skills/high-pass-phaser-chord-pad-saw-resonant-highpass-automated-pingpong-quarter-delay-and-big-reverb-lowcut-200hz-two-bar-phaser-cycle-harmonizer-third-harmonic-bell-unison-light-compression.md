# High-pass phaser chord pad: saw chords through a heavily resonant high-pass whose cutoff is automated, ping-pong 1/4 delay and a big reverb both low-cut at about 200 Hz, a phaser on a slow 2-bar cycle, a harmonizer shifted to the 3rd harmonic for a bell tone, unison and light compression

Goal: Make moving chords or a pad from a plain saw wave using a high-pass filter and a phaser (works in any synth with these, or as post-processing on a saw). Play low chords on a single saw. Add a high-pass filter with resonance turned way up and automate its cutoff to sweep the chord's movement. Add a ping-pong delay at 1/4 note with a low cut around 200 Hz (so the bass isn't muddy) and some stereo spread, then a large reverb, also low-cut about 200 Hz, with size up, slightly longer decay, damping and wet up. Add a phaser set to complete one cycle every 2 bars (its direction sets whether it sweeps up or down) for slow top-end motion. Add a harmonizer (octave copies) with amount and width high, then shift its interval from the octave to the 3rd harmonic for a bell-like tone. Add unison voices to taste, light compression, and pull the highs and some mids down a little.
