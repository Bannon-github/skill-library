2. Start the ostinato and a filtered kick from the beginning; add a building drone; bring in bassline, snare and other drums around the 1-minute mark (build-up shorter than about 2 minutes).
3. Write the held-back list: ostinato change, moving bassline, a new percussion layer (e.g. off-beat shaker), the lead, pad switching to the ostinato's harmony.
4. In the drops before the reveal, keep the bass on one note and low-cut it at times so focus stays on the ostinato.
5. In full sections solo-check each channel and mute anything not needed; automate the constant pad from about -3 dB down to about -14 dB.
6. Mark every entry or exit with a small FX hit, drum fill or (in the intro) an orchestral hit.
7. Before muting many channels at once, insert a short moment of silence.
8. At the reveal point change the ostinato for the first time, start the moving bassline, add the new percussion layer and the lead, and move the pad to the ostinato's harmony, all on the same downbeat.
9. In the following drop let the bass stop briefly while hats keep running; then switch one element every 8 bars (add, remove, or octave shift).
10. Build down slowly to a loopable outro; keep it shorter for album versions.
