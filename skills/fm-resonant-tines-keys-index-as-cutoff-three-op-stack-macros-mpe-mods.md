# FM electric-keys 'resonant tines' patch built on the subtractive analogy: modulation index as cutoff with its own envelope, a 10:1 bell ratio, a third operator with a no-sustain envelope, three macros (index, decay, ratio spread) and mod-wheel vibrato, aftertouch tremolo and an LFO auto-panner

Goal: Design a playable FM keys/tines patch by treating FM depth (modulation index) like a filter cutoff. Start with two sine operators: slow modulator gives vibrato, then speed it up to audio rate to hear sidebands. A 1:1 ratio gives a saw-like full series; 2:1 shifts the spectrum (the presenter compares it to a square) and 10:1 gives the bell/tine. Shape the carrier with a multi-stage envelope (instant attack, short transient decay, sustain-like stage, release) and the modulator depth with its own envelope that starts rich and falls slightly. Add a third modulator operator with a fast-attack, no-sustain envelope, then build macros for index (brightness), decay (all falling-stage times) and modulator ratios (spread wide in opposite directions, a formant-like control). Finish with performance modulation (mod wheel → LFO vibrato from ~3.2 rate, aftertouch → VCA tremolo, a third controller → LFO panner) and a short delay plus bright light long reverb. More specific than generic FM bell skills: macro layout and expressive modulation for a keys patch.

Source: [Multiphonics CV-3 FM Core Patch Reconstruction with Adam—Resonant Tines](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yuQqY0dx2Rw)

1. Patch one sine operator to the output with a spectrum analyser. Add a second operator at LFO rate (~8 Hz) into the first's FM input: you hear vibrato. Raise it to audio rate and key-track both from the keyboard to hear sidebands.
2. Compare ratios: 1:1 gives a saw-like spectrum with FM depth acting as cutoff; 2:1 changes the harmonic pattern (square-like per the presenter) and the harmonic tail detaches; 10:1 gives a classic FM keys/bell. Make the voice polyphonic.
3. Carrier amplitude: multi-stage envelope used like an ADSR (instant attack to max, short transient decay, a sustain-like third stage, last stage as release), gated by the keyboard.
4. Modulator depth: give the FM index its own envelope: rich at the start, falling slightly, staying fairly rich.
5. Add a third operator as an extra modulator with a fast attack and no sustain (falls to zero, a bit longer than the transient). Check the carrier stays at ratio 1 on the analyser while modulator ratios like 2 and 10 enrich the attack.
6. Macros: (1) FM index across all modulators (brightness/'cutoff'), (2) decay: the falling-stage time of all envelopes, (3) modulator ratios moved wide in opposite directions (one up high, one down low) for a formant-like colour.
7. Performance mods: mod wheel speeds an LFO from ~3.2 and sets its level into all operators' fine-tune (slight detune for tremble/vibrato); aftertouch drives a biased VCA after the carrier for tremolo; a third controller drives an LFO panner. Add a short delay and a bright, light, long reverb.
