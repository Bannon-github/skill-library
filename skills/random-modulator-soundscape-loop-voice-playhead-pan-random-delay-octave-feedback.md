# Random-modulator soundscape loop: resampled-chord drone with one random modulator on each voice's start position and pan, randomly shortened arp-driven drums, a randomly retimed delay with a +12 st pitch shifter and stepped grain size in its feedback, and a step-scanned vocal sampler

Goal: Keep a slow (88 BPM) textural loop alive using random and step modulators rather than new notes. (1) Drone: resample a plucked chord into a sampler, set it up to sustain, and have one random modulator (no smoothing; slight clicks are accepted as character) move both sample start/playhead and pan, so each voice starts somewhere different and sits elsewhere in the stereo field. Run a delay in parallel with a convolution reverb. (2) Drums: half-note mallet/brush hits from an arpeggiator whose step count is randomly shortened (16 down to a minimum of 4 steps), so the loop never repeats the same way. Send it to a delay whose time in beats is randomly modulated with smoothing (about 1–8 beats), with a pitch shifter at +12 semitones inside the feedback and a step modulator on its grain size for metallic, sideways echoes. (3) Vocal: a vocal loop in a sampler driven by an 8-step transposing arp, a 16-step modulator choosing the playhead start each step, a band-pass with a slow LFO on frequency, random pan per note, a distortion macro stepped through 16 values, then delay with reverse blur. Delta vs our shimmer and granular skills: random/step modulation of sampler playheads and delay timing as the generative engine.

Source: [Deconstructing a Textural Soundscape | Creative Sprint #5 Breakdown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uEI2Ytxn7iI)

1. Resample a plucked chord into a sampler and set it up to sustain as a drone. Add one random modulator and map it to both sample playhead/start and pan.
2. Process the drone through a standard delay in parallel with a convolution reverb.
3. Drums at slow tempo: half-note hits from a drum machine with two samples (mallet, brush) driven by an arpeggiator. Add a random modulator that shortens the arp length by up to −12 steps (16 down to 4).
4. Send drums to a delay: random modulator with smoothing on delay time in beats (about 1 to 8), a pitch shifter at +12 semitones inside the feedback loop, and a step modulator on the shifter's grain size.
5. Add a background drone layer (e.g. a pad preset with longer envelope/filter and chorus) playing the same chords lower, plus an optional texture drone into a big reverb with modulated warp, kept low in the mix.
6. Vocal layer: vocal loop in a sampler fed by an 8-step arp with transposition. Use a 16-step modulator to set a different playhead start per step, a slow LFO on a band-pass frequency, and a random modulator on pan.
7. Vocal FX: distortion preset whose saturation macro is stepped through 16 values, then a delay with reverse blur.
